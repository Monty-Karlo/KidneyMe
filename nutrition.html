Food needs are individual

Diet & Nutrition

There is no single eating plan for everyone with CKD. Nutrition needs can change as kidney disease changes, so a healthcare professional or registered dietitian can help create an individual plan.

Kidney Nutrition Basics

Sodium

Too much sodium can contribute to fluid retention, high blood pressure, and swelling. Packaged, prepared, and restaurant foods can be major sodium sources.

Potassium

CKD can make it harder for the kidneys to remove potassium. Both too much and too little potassium can cause problems, so the right amount should be based on individual needs and blood tests.

Phosphorus

Damaged kidneys may not remove phosphorus as well. Some people with CKD may need to pay closer attention to phosphorus, including phosphorus additives in processed foods.

Fluids

Some people with CKD may need to adjust fluid intake. The appropriate amount depends on kidney function and other individual factors.

Protein

Protein needs can change with CKD and dialysis. A dietitian or healthcare professional can help determine an appropriate amount and source of protein.

Read Labels

Nutrition Facts and ingredient lists can help identify sodium and added phosphorus or potassium ingredients.

Work With a Dietitian

A registered dietitian familiar with kidney disease can help build an eating plan around your health needs and preferences.

Avoid One-Size-Fits-All Advice

Do not automatically restrict potassium, phosphorus, protein, or fluids unless your healthcare team recommends it for you.

Medical Disclaimer: Kidney and Me provides educational information only. It does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Talk with a qualified healthcare professional about your individual needs.
