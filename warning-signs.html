Know when to get help

Warning Signs

Kidney disease can be difficult to notice early. New, severe, or worsening symptoms should be discussed with a healthcare professional.

Emergency symptoms

Call 911 or seek emergency medical care for severe or persistent chest pain or pressure, severe trouble breathing, fainting, or other symptoms that appear life-threatening.

Symptoms to discuss with a healthcare professional

Possible advanced CKD symptoms
  • Swelling in the legs, feet, or ankles
  • Major changes in urination
  • Persistent nausea, vomiting, or loss of appetite
  • Unusual tiredness or trouble concentrating
  • Persistent itching or muscle cramps

These symptoms can have many causes and do not by themselves diagnose kidney disease.

Possible kidney infection symptoms

Fever and chills, painful or frequent urination, cloudy or bloody urine, back/side/groin pain, and nausea or vomiting can occur with a kidney infection. NIDDK advises seeking care right away if you have symptoms of a kidney infection.

Possible sepsis symptoms

A serious infection can sometimes lead to sepsis. Warning signs can include confusion, rapid breathing or heart rate, severe pain or discomfort, fever or chills, and shortness of breath. Seek medical care right away if sepsis is suspected.

Not sure what to do? Contact your healthcare provider or a nurse advice line. If symptoms are severe or appear life-threatening, call 911.
