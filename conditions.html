Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

CKD means the kidneys are damaged or have a structural problem and cannot filter blood as well as they should. Diabetes and high blood pressure are the most common causes in adults.

Symptoms

Early CKD often has no symptoms. As CKD advances, possible symptoms include swelling, tiredness, trouble concentrating, changes in urination, nausea, itching, muscle cramps, chest pain, or shortness of breath.

Risk factors

Important risk factors include diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, and a family history of kidney failure.

Management

Management may include controlling blood pressure and blood glucose, taking medicines as prescribed, following an individualized eating plan, staying active, and monitoring kidney health with a healthcare team.

Kidney Stones

Kidney stones are solid pieces of material that form in one or both kidneys. Symptoms and treatment depend on the stone and the individual.

Kidney Failure

Kidney failure means the kidneys have lost most of their ability to function. Treatment options may include dialysis, kidney transplant, or conservative management with a healthcare team.
